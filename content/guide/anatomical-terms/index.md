The LCT-01 makes heavy usage of anatomical terms of location. It makes us sound like doctors and taxonomists, which is on-brand for who we aspire to be. Unfortunately, sewists get confused sometimes because *overlock stitch* is competing with *synapomorphy* for their limited dictionary space.

Regardless, we're here to help, because not everyone got past high school biology and its `"kingdom, phylum, class, order," (what were the four other ranks after that?)` song and dance.

* *Ventral* is the front.
  * Doctors that deal with humans often say *anterior* but we prefer *ventral*.
* *Dorsal* is the back.
  * Doctors that deal with humans often say *posterior* but we prefer *dorsal*.
* *Left* and *right* are always from your perspective; if you hug someone, your left shoulder is probably going to touch their right shoulder.
* *Inferior* and *superior* mean "below" and "above". They are not value judgements on your sewing. Don't feel bad about it; you're probably doing a great job on that sewing machine.
* *Medial* and *lateral* mean *center* and *side*. Your nose is medial, while your eyes are lateral to them; they're off to the side.
* *Exterior* and *interior* mean outside and inside respectively. The *right* side of your fabric is the *exterior* and the *wrong* side is the *interior*.

Now that we have the basic building blocks, let's combine them.

* *Ventral medial* would be *center front*. So when we speak of the *ventral medial button row* (*VMBR*), we are talking about those buttons down the front.
* *Ventral lateral* would be on the front, but to the side.
* The border between *ventral lateral* and either *left* or *right lateral* gets fuzzy because humans are not rectangles, and cows are not spheres.

### More contrived terms
We've used these terms before in our notes.

* The *coronal clavicular seam* would be the seam that holds the ventral bodice piece and dorsal bodice piece together, running between both the bodice&ndash;sleeve seam.
* That label on the back of clothing? We'd describe it as being at the superiormost medial point of the dorsal interior surface of the garment.

<br>

## Body parts

* The *transpyloric plane* is a line slightly *superior* to where your belly button / navel is. We often say that crop tops "expose the transpyloric plane".
* The *manubrium* is the top part of your sternum (breastbone), You can feel it *inferior* to your neck between your clavicles.
* The *clavicle* is a horizontal bone that connects your shoulder blade to your sternum.
