**Umerostentida** (from Latin *umerus* ("shoulder") + *ostenta* ("expose"), lit. "shoulder exposer") is one of the oldest classes in the LCT-01, dating all the way back to the system's creation in September 2025. Part of {{<taxa Scapunatoria>}}, it encompasses many common categories such as the **off-shoulder** and **cold-shoulder**.

In 2026.0005, the first major monograph, Lemuria defined Umerostentida as:

> garments that have a sleeve lateral to the upper arm while exposing at least one shoulder

In this case, a strap is a length of fabric worn superior to the clavicle, while a sleeve is lateral to the upper arm.

Umerostentida is divided into three classes, {{<taxa Alourida>}} (no straps), {{<taxa Unilourida>}} (unilateral or halterneck bilateral strap), and {{<taxa Bilourida>}} (bilateral straps). These refer to how many sides have straps; if a single side has three straps, but the other has none, that dress would be considered part of Unilourida.

## Uncertainties
The thickness of the straps in Bilourida varies. The difference between a strap, yoke, and bodice piece becomes fuzzy; typically a strap covers a small portion of the clavicle, while a bodice piece covers almost the entire clavicle and leaves only the shoulder itself exposed.
